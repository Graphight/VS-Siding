VS Siding
Thin walls and floors, built in layers

Adds thin walls and floors, a quarter of a block thick, built in layers the way a "real wall" is: a timber frame, some form of infill between the timbers, and a finish on each face. Each layer is picked separately, so you can mix and match materials. I guess I could have also called this mod "Framing", but that might have gotten confusing given the focus of this mod on the finishes.

Heavily inspired by the VS Roofing Mod. I tried to add 3D textures to the finishes that needed it too, but it turned out kind of jank (let us go with "charming"). Click a drawer below to open it.

Floors NEW

Pick Floor in the saw menu and right-click the side of a block, a deck or another floor with boards to lay joists. Right-click the top of a floor with boards to carry it on up to four blocks, which is how you get a floor out over water (sneak if you want floorboards on it instead).

  • Fill it the same way as a wall.
  • Right-click the top with a finish for floorboards, and the underside for a ceiling. Daub ceilings get lath strips.
  • Glass floors work too, and merge into one pane like glass walls. CAUTION: FLOOR MAY BE GLASS
  • A deck takes the same layers, so a floor runs right up to the wall. That is fall protection: nobody drops between storeys. OSHA would be proud.

Rugs and carpets sit on a filled floor, and lanterns, oil lamps and chandeliers hang from the underside.

Building a wall
STEP 1
Hold a saw or a stone in your off hand and the material in your main hand. Planks or sticks build the frame.
STEP 2
Right-click the face of a block with boards to place a frame.
STEP 3
Right-click the frame with an infill, then each side with a finish.

Each side takes its own finish (corners too). Breaking a wall takes off one layer at a time, outermost first, and gives the material back.

Right-click a standing wall with a stair to fill its open side beside a staircase. The step takes the stair's material and turns to match the stair next to it. A cell takes a deck or a step, not both.

No guards were removed from any saws in the making of this mod.

Saw modes

Pressing F with the saw in your off hand brings up the saw menu. It is split into rows, and each row keeps its choice. Click a pick again to turn that row off and use default behaviour.

  • Framing: Wall (a flat panel), Corner (an outside corner) or Floor (a thin floor across the top of the block). A bare frame can be turned into a corner in place.
  • Deck: a thin floor across the top of the wall, so an upper storey meets it with no gap. New frames get one as they go up, and right-clicking a standing wall with boards adds one. Right-click the deck itself to fill and finish it like a floor.
  • Boards: Weatherboard, Vertical boards or Horizontal boards (plank finishes only). On floors this sets which way the floorboards run.
  • Logs: Shakes, bare logs, vertical bark or horizontal bark (log finishes only).

Rooms and cellars

A framed and filled wall seals a room like a solid block, and a filled floor seals the room below it. Stone, clay or packed earth infill counts as a cellar wall, wattle, straw, pelt and cloth do not. Glass infill seals the room but lets daylight through. Look at a wall or floor to see its layers and whether it seals. These are also watertight.

Only wood, straw, pelt and cloth should burn. Fire takes a wall apart one layer at a time, and clay or stone infill stops it. A bone frame does not burn. This means if your house burns down and you used non-flammable infill, a husk will remain for you to easily build back up.

Signed off by the building inspector. He did not actually come out, but it sounded fine over the phone.

Furniture

A wall only fills part of its block (like 1/4), so chests, pots, torches, signs, trunks, and beds can be placed against it. The wall stays behind them and keeps sealing the room, and torches and lanterns placed against a wall light the room properly. A bed can go head-first against a wall, or laid along a straight wall (they will overhang the block next to them a little but this was nicer than phasing them into the wall). To change the wall's layers, break the furniture first.

Known issues (the snag list)
  • The lighting for interiors was a massive pain to get working properly and you might still see some weirdness, a cheeky breaking and replacing of a nearby roof block should probably fix it.
  • A floor laid right up against solid ground can show a dark strip on its underside where it meets the ground. You only see it if you crawl into the gap under it, so I have left it alone.
  • Decks built before 1.3.0 now show bare joists instead of a solid slab. They still seal, and filling them closes them up again.
  • A torch or lantern set into a wall also lights the ground outside that wall. A block or slab against the outside stops it until I fix it.
